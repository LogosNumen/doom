Ｂｅｆｏｒｅ　ｙｏｕ　ｂｅｇｉｎ

This manual describes a machine. Whether the machine described is the machine in front of you is a question this manual is not equipped to answer, and the question comes up more often than you would like.

Read the whole thing before touching anything. Then read it again, because on the second pass you will notice that the sections do not agree with each other, and knowing which parts disagree is more useful than believing any of them.

If you were sent here from the login: this page will not tell you the phrase. It will tell you something you need in order to work it out, which is different, and worse.

Ｔｈｅ　ｆｒｏｎｔ　ｐａｎｅｌ

Left to right: the meter, the coarse control, the fine control, the lamp, and one unlabelled toggle.

The meter reads out of a circuit that was cut before delivery. It moves. Nobody has established what moves it.

The coarse and fine controls are geared together at a ratio that is printed nowhere. Turning the fine control past its stop turns the coarse control by one increment, which means the machine has no upper limit, which means the scale printed on the front is decorative.

Do not use the unlabelled toggle. Not because of what it does — because after you have used it you will not be able to stop thinking about what it did.

Ｒｅｇｉｓｔｅｒｓ

Eight registers. Six are factory-set and sealed. Two are yours.

R0   CARRIER       sealed   — always 1. it has never been 0.
R1   DWELL         sealed   — 40, in minutes
R2   GROUP LENGTH  sealed   — 5
R3   OFFSET        sealed   — 07. subtract it. do not add it.
R4   REPEAT        sealed   — 2
R5   PREAMBLE      sealed   — 12 seconds of tone
R6   —               yours    — blank
R7   —               yours    — blank

R3 is the register people write in about. It is an offset, in the ordinary sense: the amount by which the thing you are looking at differs from the thing you want. The transmitted value is always high by R3. Take R3 off it.

The two blank registers were blank when the machine arrived and there is no documented way to write to them. I mention them because there is a scratch on the paint under R7, of the kind a fingernail makes.

Ｉｆ　ｔｈｅ　ｌａｍｐ　ｉｓ　ｌｉｔ

Steady: normal.

Blinking, thirty a minute: normal.

Blinking, forty a minute: also normal. Nothing in the machine is capable of forty a minute.

Out: the machine is running correctly and the lamp has failed. There is no condition in which the lamp being out means the machine has stopped. The machine does not have a way to stop.

Ｆａｕｌｔｓ

No output.  Not a fault. Check the meter, which is not connected.

Output when the machine is off.  Not a fault. See DWELL.

Wrong output.  There is no wrong output. There is output you were not expecting, which is a different section of a different manual.

Voice on the channel.  Log the time. Do not answer. Answering has never made it stop and on one occasion is understood to have made it worse.

Two lamps.  There is one lamp.

Ｍａｉｎｔｅｎａｎｃｅ

Annually: check the guy wires, replace the lamp, sweep the floor of the hut.

The lamp does not need replacing. Replace it anyway. It is the only task on this list that anybody has ever actually completed and there is a value in that which the schedule does not capture.

There is no procedure for shutting the station down. There was one. It is not in this copy, and the page numbers run continuously, so it was never in this copy.
